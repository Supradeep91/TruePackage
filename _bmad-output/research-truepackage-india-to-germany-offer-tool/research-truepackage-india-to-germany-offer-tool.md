# Market research: India-Germany decision-product positioning

**Decision this research serves:** Choose an umbrella value proposition and segment-specific first-screen proof for people studying, job seeking, taking offers, or returning between India and Germany.

## Executive Summary

**Recommendation:** Position TruePackage as an **India↔Germany move decision tool**, not a generic salary calculator. Use one umbrella promise, then let visitors choose the decision they face: move for work, return to India, study in Germany, or job-seek in Germany.

The broad promise “one number for whether Germany makes financial sense” is no longer a clear category gap. dual.tax compares India/Germany net pay and purchasing power [2]; PPPCalculator.info's guide explicitly frames an offer against a PPP-equivalent threshold [5]; ParityDeals compares city-level PPP and costs [6]; Indian in Germany and NRI Tools already tailor net-pay information for Indian users [3][7]. These are observed product-page capabilities, not proof of traffic, accuracy, or customer preference.

The product's stronger distinction is the **decision workflow**: turn a person's route, city, household, compensation, and commitments into the next useful number or action. For professionals that may be sustainable net pay and a counter; for students, upfront funds and a monthly gap; for job seekers, investment and runway; for returners, a target Indian CTC. The current landing page makes this hard to see: a generic tax hook leads, while the mode selector, decision output, and study/job-seeker value arrive later [9][10]. The visual system itself is coherent; the early-stage impression comes more from hierarchy and trust details than from color or type.

**Suggested umbrella positioning:** “Make your India↔Germany move with the numbers clear.” Supporting line: “Choose your path to see the take-home, upfront funds, salary target, or monthly gap that matters for your city and situation.” Each journey should then name its own job and outcome, rather than forcing one universal benefit claim.

**Why choose it over an LLM?** Not because LLMs categorically cannot calculate. The product has to be the lower-effort, more inspectable instrument: structured inputs for each route, consistent scenario updates, explicit data-year and assumptions, a calculation trail, and a short action-oriented result. Those are product requirements and hypotheses to test, not established reasons users already choose TruePackage. Also, the current “Share these numbers” button does not preserve changed inputs in the URL [10], so shareability is not yet a working differentiator.

**Biggest caveat:** Current first-person user evidence is weak. Reddit pages and search APIs returned 403, and no interviews were conducted. Validate the message across all four segments before claiming this is the category's dominant pain.

## User-Provided Segment Model

On 2026-10-30, the product owner clarified four intended audiences: people moving from India to Germany or Germany to India; students exploring study in Germany; professionals deciding whether an offer is sustainable in a chosen city; and job seekers evaluating return on investment and required upfront funds. The owner expects the relevant value mix to vary by segment: decision clarity, accurate calculations, speed, shareability, and actionable guidance may all matter, but not in the same order. The page should show enough to earn trust without overwhelming visitors.

This is product-owner direction, not independent user research. It supports a four-journey information architecture, but it does not establish segment size, priority, or which benefit converts each audience.

**Suggested first-screen structure:** a short umbrella promise followed by four clear choices, each with one question and one outcome:

- **Moving to Germany for work:** “What will I take home, and what salary makes this city sustainable?”
- **Returning to India:** “What CTC keeps my chosen lifestyle?”
- **Studying in Germany:** “How much must I arrange before arrival, and what is my monthly funding gap?”
- **Job seeking in Germany:** “What will the move cost, how long is my runway, and what offer should I target?”

After the visitor chooses, show one primary result, one reason to trust it, and one next action. Put assumptions, tax detail, cost breakdowns, and alternative scenarios behind progressive disclosure. This is a recommended design hypothesis, not a validated conversion result.

## Target Job and Pain

There is no single job to be done across the audience. The common umbrella is a consequential India↔Germany decision with financial uncertainty; the actual job differs by route: evaluate a work offer, plan a return, fund study, or price the job search. Competitor pages organize their products around net pay, India/Germany purchasing power, Indian-rupee context, cost of living, and relocation planning [2][3][6][7][8]. This is evidence of how publishers package the problem, not evidence that users prefer one job over another.

Search discovery surfaced offer-evaluation and family-budget threads, but original posts were inaccessible and dates could not be verified. They are excluded from the findings. **User-voice confidence is low:** recent interviews or accessible, dated first-person sources are needed before segment or messaging claims are treated as validated.

## Landing-Page Audit

At a 1024×640 capture of the live page, the first screen is mostly a centered hero: “Your offer looks great. Until tax hits,” generic supporting copy (“Built for Indians navigating Germany...”), and a “Start calculating” button. The calculator starts at the lower edge; the personalized decision appears further down [9]. The visual treatment is restrained and consistent. The “student-built” impression is better explained by the message and proof sequence: the tool's most valuable answer is hidden behind a broad hook, and the first screen could belong to almost any tax calculator.

Two trust/clarity issues compound that problem:

- The hero claims Germans keep 52–57% of gross, while the visible default example says 49% reaches the user's account [9]. These may refer to different cases, but on one screen they read as conflicting. Use a case-specific, dynamically computed claim or remove the range.
- The decision state can say “Good offer — counter to €100K” and then “Accept. Counter to €100K if there is room.” The two actions are mixed. Separate offer status, minimum-to-accept, and negotiation target in the copy and visual hierarchy. The implementation is in [src/App.tsx](../../src/App.tsx).
- The “Share these numbers” button copies `window.location.href`, but `writeParams` is never called anywhere in `src/`; the hook only reads initial query parameters and exposes an unwired write function [10]. Changed calculator inputs therefore are not encoded for sharing. Fix and test this before promoting shareability as a product advantage.
- The visible footer says “Data: June 2026,” while the repository's data-source register says it was last updated in March 2026 [9][11]. Reconcile this provenance, or clearly state which underlying datasets were refreshed when. A transparent calculation story only helps if its dates agree.

The product already exposes four journeys and relevant calculations: Germany/India compensation comparison, student funding, and job-seeker runway/upfront funds [10]. The issue is not lack of breadth; it is that the page currently asks visitors to infer which flow fits them and why the outputs are dependable.

## Cross-Dimension Insight

The category is crowded at the **ingredient** level and less clearly differentiated at the **decision workflow** level. Net salary, INR conversion, country/city comparisons, PPP, visa guidance, and savings estimates are all visible elsewhere [1]–[8]. TruePackage should not claim exclusivity for those components or for the existence of a “Germany number.” Its defensible position is an integrated, segment-aware path from a real move scenario to the next decision: offer threshold, return CTC, study funding gap, or job-search runway.

The LLM comparison is not “AI versus calculator.” LLMs are useful for open-ended follow-up questions; TruePackage should win when a visitor wants to get a structured, repeatable, inspectable answer without designing a prompt or assembling multiple sources. To earn that claim, it must demonstrate clear inputs, current sources, stable calculations, relevant scenario switching, and a useful next action. Any claim that an LLM is inaccurate or cannot do the same work needs a current, controlled comparison; this run did not perform one.

## Recommendations

1. **Use an umbrella promise plus four explicit journeys.** Give work migration, return to India, study, and job seeking equal first-screen recognition unless later evidence justifies a priority order. Do not list every feature; use one decision question and outcome per route.
2. **Progressively disclose detail.** After a route is chosen, show its primary number, one trust signal (tax/data year, sources, or assumptions), and one next action. Keep tax lines, budget components, and what-if scenarios expandable.
3. **Make the LLM contrast a product demonstration.** Test a real scenario in TruePackage and an LLM. Measure time to a usable result, missing inputs, assumption visibility, consistency when changing city/family/salary, and whether users can act on or reproduce the result. Avoid claims that LLMs cannot calculate or are inaccurate without evidence.
4. **Fix scenario sharing before advertising it.** Connect the URL writer to every relevant input and route, then verify copying/opening the URL in a fresh browser reproduces the same result. Today the button only copies the URL and the writer is unused [10].
5. **Repair trust details.** Reconcile the 52–57% headline with the 49% example; separate offer status, minimum-to-accept, and counter target; align the data dates shown in the UI and source register [9][10][11].
6. **Use intent-specific acquisition pages.** Build high-intent entry pages around the four actual questions rather than generic “Germany salary calculator” copy. Measure which route attracts qualified users; this is a go-to-market hypothesis, not a search-volume finding.
7. **Validate with each segment before optimizing a universal headline.** Interview 5–8 users per segment or run moderated concept tests. Ask them to paraphrase the value, identify what they would verify, and complete one real scenario. Test the segment chooser and LLM comparison with task completion and trust measures, not preference alone.

## Open Questions

- Should all four segments receive equal first-screen weight, or should product analytics and interviews later identify a lead segment?
- Which one or two proof points matter most within each journey, given the owner's expectation that all five value dimensions apply differently by segment?
- In a controlled task, do users reach a reliable, usable decision faster in TruePackage than with an LLM or a combination of existing tools?
- Can the threshold logic and city-cost assumptions be shown transparently enough to earn trust against existing calculators?
- Will users value persistent shareable scenarios? The current URL button does not encode changed inputs [10].
- What does Desi Expat's calculator actually do? Its first-party page was unavailable during this run.
- What do recent users say about this decision? Direct Reddit access failed; conduct interviews or use accessible, dated user research before treating pain themes as established.
