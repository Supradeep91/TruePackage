# First-party student-tool verification digest, round 1

**Accessed:** 2026-10-30

## Findings

- **[claim, source, publisher, pub_date, accessed, confidence, class]** The Study Abroad Compass describes a seven-factor destination comparison (academic fit, cost, language/culture, career outcomes, safety/wellbeing, visa ease, and lifestyle), with 0–10 user-weight sliders and a personalized shortlist. It explicitly labels prefilled country data illustrative and tells users to verify real figures. This directly overlaps the concept of user-weighted study comparison, but is a destination-level framework rather than verified program outcomes. **Source:** https://studyabroadcompass.org/tools/country-comparison; **publisher:** The Study Abroad Compass; **pub_date:** undated, ©2026; **accessed:** 2026-10-30; **confidence:** high for observed flow; **class:** competitor capability/data caveat.
- **[claim, source, publisher, pub_date, accessed, confidence, class]** UniPilot AI advertises AI matching from grades, budget, target country, and priorities; match scores/eligibility estimates; application tracking; document storage; and country-specific checklists across 12 destinations. It lists a last-updated date of 2026-05-10, but the inspected homepage did not expose a detailed scoring methodology or external validation of match scores. **Source:** https://unipilotai.com/; **publisher:** UniPilot AI; **pub_date:** 2026-05-10 (site-stated); **accessed:** 2026-10-30; **confidence:** high for advertised features, low for score validity; **class:** competitor capability/positioning.
- **[claim, source, publisher, pub_date, accessed, confidence, class]** AdmissionsPedia advertises free/no-signup admissions tools, university search/comparison, city cost, and application roadmaps for 150+ countries. Its page says city-cost coverage includes 4,800+ cities and names College Scorecard, QS, THE, official university portals, Kaggle/Numbeo, and World Bank PPP as sources. It also displays selected beta-user testimonials; these are vendor-published and not independently verified. **Source:** https://www.admissionspedia.com/; **publisher:** AdmissionsPedia; **pub_date:** undated, ©2026; **accessed:** 2026-10-30; **confidence:** high for public page claims, not for outcome accuracy; **class:** competitor capability.
- **[claim, source, publisher, pub_date, accessed, confidence, class]** FreeStudentTools presents a side-by-side university comparison with tuition, city cost, entry requirements, scholarships, and post-graduation outcomes, and says it sources data from official university pages. Its page says data is updated regularly but does not state per-field freshness. **Source:** https://freestudenttools.com/compare; **publisher:** FreeStudentTools; **pub_date:** undated, ©2026; **accessed:** 2026-10-30; **confidence:** high for disclosed product behavior; **class:** competitor capability/data provenance.
- **[claim, source, publisher, pub_date, accessed, confidence, class]** Study-Abroad.org's comparison page organizes destination/city/program content across tuition, living costs, visas, post-study work, and career outcomes; the inspected page lists 19 editorial comparison guides. It is an information product, not evidence of a shared normalized interactive score. **Source:** https://www.study-abroad.org/compare/; **publisher:** Study-Abroad.org / Arrimundo; **pub_date:** linked articles 2026-03 to 2026-04; **accessed:** 2026-10-30; **confidence:** high for observed page structure; **class:** competing information product.

## Implication and evidence limits

A standalone “AI university finder/ranker” is not a whitespace opportunity by itself. Existing products advertise weighted destination matching, AI profile matching, or sourced university comparisons. A plausible differentiation hypothesis is more rigorous decision provenance: factor-level sources and dates, clear comparability coverage, user-adjustable weights, and an honest “insufficient data” result. No evidence here demonstrates that users prefer this approach, that competitor scores are inaccurate, or that users would pay for a new entrant.
